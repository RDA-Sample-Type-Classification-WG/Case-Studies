# Samples in the Neutron Imaging community

## Samples in the Neutron Imaging community

We deal with a very broad variety of samples.  These range from
engineering materials such as metals, alloys, and composites, to
energy-related systems like batteries and fuel cells, as well as
geological samples (rocks, soils), cultural heritage objects,
biological specimens, and paleontological samples.  The samples can
vary significantly in size and complexity, from small laboratory
specimens to large, heterogeneous objects.  Often, the samples are
functional devices, such as novel 3D printers, running engines, or
other machinery under investigation, where the sample is the process
itself.

## Characterising properties

Samples in neutron imaging are distinguished by their composition,
internal structure, and interaction with neutrons.  Key properties
include sensitivity to light elements (e.g. hydrogen, lithium),
differences in neutron attenuation, and crystallographic or
phase-related features.  In addition, samples vary significantly in
size (<1mm to >1meter), geometry, and degree of heterogeneity.

Very often, we study complete devices under operando conditions, where
the process itself is the subject of investigation.  In such cases,
custom-built sample environments become a central part of the
experiment and can effectively be considered part of the sample.
Compared to other communities, neutron imaging enables non-destructive
investigation of bulk materials, with particular sensitivity to light
elements, which sets it apart from techniques such as X-ray imaging.
