## Samples in the material science community

*Describe the samples that you are dealing with in your community.
Give an overview of the variety of different samples.*

Synthesized and processed versions of (with more specific examples in parantheses): Metals and alloys (pure metals, binary alloys, ternary alloys, multicomponent alloys, steels, stainless steels, tool steels, aluminum alloys, titanium alloys, nickel alloys, cobalt alloys, magnesium alloys, copper alloys, refractory metals, superalloys, metallic glasses, high-entropy alloys), Ceramics (oxides, carbides, nitrides, borides, silicides, glass-ceramics, structural ceramics, electroceramics, bioceramics, ultra-high-temperature ceramics), Polymers (thermoplastics, thermosets, elastomers, hydrogels, conductive polymers, biopolymers, copolymers, polymer blends, filled polymers), Composites (fiber-reinforced polymers, metal-matrix composites, ceramic-matrix composites, particulate composites, laminates, sandwich structures, nanocomposites, hybrid composites), Semiconductors (elemental semiconductors, compound semiconductors, wide-bandgap semiconductors, organic semiconductors, perovskite semiconductors, doped wafers, epitaxial layers), Soft materials (colloids, gels, foams, liquid crystals, surfactant systems, biological soft matter), Biomaterials (implant alloys, bioceramics, bioactive glasses, biodegradable polymers, tissue scaffolds, drug-delivery materials), Energy materials (battery electrodes, solid electrolytes, fuel-cell membranes, catalysts, thermoelectrics, photovoltaic absorbers, hydrogen-storage materials), Quantum, magnetic, and electronic materials (ferromagnets, ferrites, spintronic materials, superconductors, dielectrics, piezoelectrics, ferroelectrics, multiferroics), Carbon-based materials (graphite, graphene, carbon fibers, carbon black, activated carbon, diamond, diamond-like carbon coatings, carbon nanotubes).

## Characterising properties

*What are the properties that can be used to distinguish different
types of samples being used in your domain?  What properties
distinguish them from samples in other domains?*

A list that probably forgets some things: Optical microscopy, stereo microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, transmission electron microscopy, focused ion beam analysis, atomic force microscopy, scanning probe microscopy, scanning tunneling microscopy, electron backscatter diffraction, electron probe microanalysis, energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, wavelength-dispersive spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, small- and wide-angle X-ray scattering, electron diffraction, neutron diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy, UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy, fluorescence spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, Auger electron spectroscopy, secondary ion mass spectrometry, nuclear magnetic resonance, mass spectrometry, Hall and four-point-probe electrical measurements, dielectric characterization, magnetic measurements, mechanical testing such as tensile, compression, flexure, fracture, fatigue, creep, hardness, and nanoindentation, thermal analysis such as DSC, TGA, DMA, dilatometry, and thermal conductivity or diffusivity measurements, surface and interface characterization such as profilometry, ellipsometry, contact angle, and adhesion testing, porosity and particle characterization such as gas adsorption, mercury porosimetry, pycnometry, particle size analysis, and zeta potential, tomography and nondestructive evaluation such as X-ray CT and ultrasonic methods, and electrochemical characterization such as cyclic voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, galvanostatic cycling, and corrosion testing.
