# Samples in the Neutron Scattering community

## About the Neutron Scattering community

We represent the Neutron Scattering Working Group at IQMT, KIT.  We
focus on investigating the structure and lattice dynamics of solids
using elastic and inelastic neutron scattering, complemented by hard
x-ray scattering at large-scale user facilities.  The scientific scope
includes phonons, charge-ordered materials, transition-metal
compounds, and superconducting systems.

## Samples in the Neutron Scattering community

Our community mainly works with inorganic crystalline materials,
predominantly single crystals.  Polycrystalline samples and powders
are also investigated in some cases.  In addition, nanostructured
materials and nanoscale systems can be studied when required.

## Characterising properties

Samples in our community are primarily distinguished by their physical
form and structural order, including single crystals, mosaics of
aligned single crystals, thin films, polycrystalline materials,
powders, and nanostructured systems such as nanoparticles.

Further distinguishing properties include sample mass, chemical
composition, and crystallographic symmetry, as well as sensitivity to
air, moisture, or other external conditions, and possible
radioactivity.  In addition, the presence or absence of hydrogen in a
sample is an important criterion, since hydrogen has a very large
incoherent neutron scattering cross section, leading to strong
background and reduced signal quality in neutron scattering
experiments.  These properties determine sample preparation, handling,
and experimental constraints.
