# Samples in the synchrotron physical sciences community

## About the synchrotron physical sciences community

This is extremely broad, and includes communities already described
under 'condensed matter physics', 'magnetism' and 'materials science',
in addition to a broad range of disciplines that includes chemistry,
engineering science, surface science and much more.  Despite the broad
range of science, these communities intersect at central facilities,
from which some commonality can be found.  For example, the
international photon and neutron community makes extensive use of the
NeXus metadata model, which provides a rudimentary framework for
sample descriptions.

## Samples in the synchrotron physical sciences community

These are, again, very wide ranging.  They include, for example:

### Ephemeral samples

These may or may not exist prior to the experiment. They can be grown
or modified during the measurement.  For example, studies of thin
films during growth.

### Continuous samples

Samples of liquids and gasses are effectively continuous.  Individual
samples represent some quantity of the parent substance.

### Unique samples

These are samples where there exists a single individual in the sample
type/class. For example, unique archaeological artefacts or
engineering structures.

Often, samples in the physical sciences can be described quite well in
terms of their chemical composition and phase, along with their
crystallographic/magnetic structures, where relevant.  These are
typically dependent on the phase of the sample, which may depend on
thermodynamic variables and history.

## Characterising properties

Again, extremely broad.  The relevant sample characteristics depend on
the sub-community that is carrying out the study.

## Vocabularies and metadata standards

Global standards are not in widespread use, for example at Diamond
Light Source, except for the quite generic and arbitrary metadata
contained in the NXsample NeXus definition:

https://manual.nexusformat.org/classes/base_classes/NXsample.html

Individual sample identifiers are widely for high throughput
experiments, but they tend to have only locally-meaningful semantics
or arbitrary string descriptions.

There is considerable interest to move beyond this situation, and
provide formal descriptions of sample types, underpinned by community
taxonomies and ontologies.  These identifiers and schemes would then
be common to different services at a facility, and provide
globally-agreed semantics and machine actionability beyond the
facility.
