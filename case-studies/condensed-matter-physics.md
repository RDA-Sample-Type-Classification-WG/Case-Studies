# Samples in the condensed matter physics community

## About the condensed matter physics community

The condensed matter physics with focus on magnetism is studying
structure and interactions within magnetic materials.  Often times, it
is also studied in extreme environment such as low temperatures,
magnetic fields or high pressures.  The research helps with
fundamental understanding of various mechanisms, quantum phenomena, as
well as engineering of new materials with potential for renewable
energies or data storage.

## Samples in the condensed matter physics community

There are two basic groups - polycrystalline (powder) samples and
single crystals.  The single crystal samples usually provide more
details about the system as some properties might be linked to the
crystallographic directions in the material and there is lower risk of
contamination/additional effects from impurities.  Another category,
that can be included here, are thin films.  Majority of research in
the M-CM community are inorganic materials, but recently, there was
increased interest in organic samples too.  When it comes to physics
itself, there are two classes - magnetism of d- and f-elements.
Additionally, there are many subcategories of materials -
superconductors, frustrated magnets, multiferroic materials, spin
liquids, etc.

## Characterising properties

Primarily, they should be either magnetic or have some interesting
spin-orbital entanglement that makes them a good candidates for
investigation with neutron scattering and/or synchrotron radiation.

shape: sphere, cube, powder, thin film..."

## Vocabularies and metadata standards

There are crystallographic standards, when it comes to the structure
of samples.  One database that contains various compounds is
international crystallographic database ICSD.  There is also attempt
to catalogue the magnetic structures on Bilbao crystallographic
server.  However, it is much smaller database due to limited personnel
and, perhaps, effort from scientists from the M-CM community too.

## Sample PIDs being used

I am not aware of any type of sample identification (apart from
cataloguing numbering in databases mentioned above, but it is usually
not used as a reference).
